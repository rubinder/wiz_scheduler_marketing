When I Work is a capable scheduling tool, and its auto-assign comes with every plan. It can require a shift to overlap at least 70% with an employee's preferred availability, and it respects maximum hours. It has no coworker preferences. Wiz Scheduler goes further on what employees can ask for: preferred days, preferred hours, a weekly hour limit, and who they work well with or should be kept apart from go into the generator, and the week is built around them.

## How preferences work

Managers can enter preferences for everyone: preferred days, preferred time ranges, a weekly limit on hours, a cap on shifts in a time range, times someone can't work, and coworkers to work with, keep apart from, or never schedule together. Turn on preference requests in Company Settings and your employees can send their preferences for you to approve. Requests are off by default, and a manager approves each one and sets how much it counts.

A firm preference means the employee is not considered for a shift that breaks it, and the shift is left open if nobody else qualifies. A soft one makes the employee a less likely pick, and if they get the shift anyway it is marked with an asterisk. That is why there is less to fix: the preferences are already in the draft, and the places it couldn't meet them are marked.

## Pricing

When I Work charges per user, so the price grows with every hire. Wiz Scheduler charges one base price for the company, with every location included.

## Where When I Work is stronger

- **A low per-user price for one location.** The one-location rate is {wiwOne} per user.
- **Preferred availability with a threshold.** It can require 70% overlap with an employee's preferred availability and respects maximum hours.

If those are the reasons you use When I Work, it may well remain the better fit.
